# Fuel patches and corpse detection

## October 18 visual revision

The later author correction restores the earlier procedural fireplace material for both attached and ground flames. Original native mask/noise textures are retained. Renderer-owned noise scales/speeds and particle width/height, lean and lifetime provide variation without a shared material change. Atlas slicing and the custom rounded mask are not used. Upper-body attachment, red corpse tint and fifteen-second minimum patch lifetime are retained. The following description of the rejected Flame1 atlas revision is historical; current evidence is in TESTING.md.

The author rejected the large procedural fire quads as blocky. Enemy and deposited-fuel fire now use private copies of the existing native animated Flame1 atlas material. Six small upper-body emitters have varied sizes, random atlas start frames, short varying lifetimes and distinct simulation rates. The upper-body anchors select nearby animated skin bones once, retain offsets and stay outside the native NPC hierarchy. Ground fire varies particle sizes/lifetimes/phases within a bounded emitter. No shared random sequence or native material is changed. The generated lower-edge mask was removed. These are authored/engine-checked changes; final appearance remains a live check.

The author chose a fifteen-second minimum burning patch lifetime, exposed as Minimum burn seconds. More deposited fuel extends this; refuelling refreshes the minimum. Patches consume their remaining simulated fuel over the computed deadline, including the last fraction, then shrink/fade and clean up. The earlier fixed 0.01 L/s description below is historical; that rate now determines extra duration above the minimum. The author then explicitly retained the coal atlas and requested flesh-red pale regions. Only corpse-owned material colour multiplication changes those pale cracks; black regions stay dark and the source texture is untouched.

Optional BloodFX support is signature-checked and discovered from already loaded BepInEx plugins. It has no hard assembly reference, redistributed DLL, private-repair requirement or configuration-file modification. A supported enabled and updating instance receives exact paid chainsaw contacts for both LMB/RMB and corpses; otherwise native standalone feedback remains. Corpses use BloodFX's native HitCorpse to preserve its cooldown, intensity, count multiplier and pool settings. While our own weapon is selected, only BloodFX's click-guessing path is skipped. Exact owned thermal/handled-solid Health debits are subtracted from its next polling effect, preserving unrelated damage in that poll and clearing accounting after the poll. Unknown contracts warn once and retain standalone behavior. Native damage amounts/events, stock input and foreign config are unchanged. This adapter uses private methods because BloodFX 0.2 exposes no public hit API; changed future signatures safely fall back.

The 1.6.3 candidate replaces the flamethrower's private sight alignment with RMB cold fuel spray. It does not change the approved hip grip, native firearm aim settings, shared input FSMs or placements. LMB takes priority when both mouse buttons are held. Both modes debit the actual continuous tank balance, including its final fraction, once per frame. Misses still consume fuel.

RMB uses the posed nozzle and the same cover/window-aware curved trajectory as the flame stream. Deposits require an upward solid surface; bodies, triggers, water, invalid coordinates and steep surfaces are rejected. A surface-projected mesh represents each roughly 0.55 m radius patch. Nearby deposits on the same collider/fuel type merge, with at most 0.3 L per patch and 64 patches. Excess fuel is consumed rather than creating unbounded hazards. No native collider, item hierarchy, material or save format is modified.

LMB surface contact ignites deposited fuel. Touching patches propagate ignition at 0.15 seconds per link. Solid line of sight, compatible surface normals/heights and supported intermediate ground points are required; footprints cannot bridge empty ledges or a wall. Different fuel types can connect. A fresh deposit touching burning fuel also catches.

Each patch retains its selected fuel's configured DPS. Ground contacts use native root Bodypart damage and apply ignite. Overlapping patches choose the highest ground damage per actor per tick; the flame stream remains an independent additive source and keeps its own paid-damage queue. Ignite contact intervals are combined so standing in a patch while receiving the stream does not double the agreed one-stack-per-second buildup. Ground fuel burns at 0.01 L/s; unlit fuel evaporates after 60 scaled seconds by default. Pause stops consumption and damage. Switching weapons leaves deposited hazards active through the existing persistent runtime runner. Scene changes clear them. Patches are transient and are not saved.

Attached enemy flames move up 0.15 m and use a private procedural rounded-bottom mask where the current native fire shader supports its mask property. Native shared flame materials remain unchanged. Only the mask is generated by this code; no extracted texture was added to distribution. Rendered edge softness and droplet/fire appearance require a live check.

## Native death contract

Stock Health states can transition to corpse creation below 1 HP. Testing only Health <= 0 missed deaths leaving a positive fraction. The candidate reads an enabled FloatCompare on the current Health state, requires the actual Health variable, a finite constant and zero tolerance, and follows its local event transition to a qualified physical corpse CreateObject. It neither changes native Health/death actions nor installs an NPC-name allowlist. Ambiguous or unsupported custom contracts retain the conservative zero-health fallback.

A private read-only asset audit examined 51 native Health/death definitions, including humans, hounds, lizards, spiders, mutants and bosses. Physical `*_Dead` prefabs are identified independently of species. Some native creatures explode/disappear without a physical corpse; no corpse is invented for them. Exact CreateObject interception still captures the body before loot overwrites the native Item output. Only corpse-owned material copies change. Corpse appearance persistence across save/reload remains unverified.
